Drains and sewer lines

Slow tubs, backed-up floor drains and sewage smells usually start here. A plumber may clear a clog with drain cleaning or use high-pressure water in hydro jetting for grease and roots.

For the main line, sewer line cleaning and sewer line repair handle the pipe to the street, while a sewer inspection uses a camera to see what is wrong first. A slow drain in one fixture is often a local clog, while several slow drains at once point to the main line. Say which one you have when you call.

Water heaters

A tank unit often lasts roughly 8 to 12 years, so many calls are about a unit near the end of its life. Choose water heater repair for a failed element or valve, or water heater replacement when the tank itself leaks.

New setups go through water heater installation. If you want endless hot water and a smaller footprint, ask about a tankless water heater. Sediment, scale and a worn anode rod are common reasons tanks fail, so ask the plumber what they find inside before you decide.

Leaks and pipes

Hidden leaks show up as high bills or damp spots. Leak detection finds the source without opening walls blindly, and burst pipe repair is the emergency answer when a line fails.

Bigger jobs include the water service line from the street and whole-house repiping. Basements and crawlspaces fall under sump pump work and basement waterproofing. If you see water on a floor and cannot tell where it comes from, shut off the main valve first. That stops a supply leak and helps you tell supply water from drain water.

Fixtures and installs

Running toilets, dripping faucets and jammed disposals are common and usually fast to fix. See toilet repair, faucet repair and garbage disposal repair.

Remodels and new appliances need plumbing installation. Hard water complaints lead to water softener and filtration questions, and a plumber can help you decide whether you need one.
