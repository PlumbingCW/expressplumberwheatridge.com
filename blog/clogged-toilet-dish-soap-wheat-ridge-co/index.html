The Clogged Toilet Dish Soap Trick: A Wheat Ridge, CO Guide

A squirt of dish soap and a pot of hot water clears a surprising number of toilet clogs. It also has limits, and knowing them saves you from a bigger mess.

By the Wheat Ridge Express Plumbing team

Plumber on call
(303) 552-3896
Call Now

The clogged toilet dish soap trick is a low-cost first move that works on soft blockages: too much toilet paper, a waste plug that is stuck but not packed hard. Dish soap coats the pipe wall and the clog, so the mass slides along more easily when water pushes behind it.

It will not dissolve a toy, a wipe or a hardened plug, and it does nothing for a blockage deep in the main line. This post sorts out which clogs respond, gives the exact steps, and shows where to draw the line before the water in the bowl rises past the rim.

Try this first

The dish soap method, one safe round at a time

1

Stop flushing

Do not flush again. If the bowl is near the rim, lift the tank lid and close the flapper by hand, or shut the supply valve behind the toilet.

2

Add the soap

Squeeze about a quarter cup of liquid dish soap into the bowl, aiming at the drain opening, and let it sink for 10 to 15 minutes.

3

Pour hot water

Heat a pot of water until it is steaming but not boiling, then pour it from waist height. Boiling water can crack porcelain.

4

Wait and test

Give it 10 minutes. If the level drops, flush once to check. If not, move to the plunger.

Tap water from the hot faucet is a gentler substitute if you have a big, fragile older bowl. Use a bucket and pour gradually instead of sending it all down at once, so you do not overflow.

Why it works and where it stops working

A clog is often soft paper wadded against the trap, the S-shaped bend inside the toilet. Soap reduces friction and the warm water softens the paper. The two together can loosen a plug enough for gravity to finish the job.

Anything that does not soften will sit where it is. That includes baby wipes sold as flushable, cotton swabs, feminine products, dental floss and small toys. Soap cannot help with these, and warm water can make a tight wipe wad swell.

If you have waited twice and the water still does not move, stop pouring in more. Extra water in a fully blocked bowl is the usual recipe for an overflow onto the floor.

Plunger first, then the closet auger

A flange plunger, with the extra rubber lip that fits the toilet outlet, works much better than the flat sink cup. Seat it so the lip sits in the drain, then push gently the first time to push out air. Pump 10 to 15 times with steady force, then pull back sharply to break the seal.

A closet auger is a hand-cranked cable inside a rigid tube, with a rubber sleeve that protects the porcelain. Feed it into the drain, crank clockwise and push until you feel the clog give. Pull it out slowly and wipe it down.

Wear gloves and put down towels. If the auger meets a hard stop, or comes out with something that does not belong, you may be dealing with a toilet repair problem like a loose wax ring or a cracked trap rather than an ordinary clog.

Safety

What not to put in a clogged toilet

Never do this

  • Pour chemical drain cleaners into standing water
  • Mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaner
  • Use boiling water in a cold, cracked bowl
  • Reach into the drain with a coat hanger

Better choices

  • Plunger with a flange
  • Closet auger with a sleeve
  • Dish soap and warm water
  • A plumber with a drain machine

Chemical cleaners can eat pipe fittings, and they leave a hazard that burns skin and eyes for whoever works on the blockage next. Mixing products can release toxic gas. If a chemical is already in the bowl, tell the plumber before anyone touches it.

What to do before you call for help

Do not keep flushing. A second flush into a blocked bowl is the usual cause of an overflow. Lift the tank lid and close the flapper, or turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop refill.

Do not mix drain chemicals into standing water, and never combine cleaners. Splashback from caustic liquid burns skin and eyes. When to call a plumber: the bowl rises again after plunging, other drains gurgle, or water shows at a floor drain.

Model codes such as the UPC and IPC set how piping, venting and fixtures are installed, though the version in force can differ by jurisdiction, so check with the Building Division before work starts.

Bigger problem

When one clogged toilet is really a main line issue

A toilet is the lowest-pressure fixture in the house, so it often shows trouble first. Watch for these combined signs.

More than one fixture is slow

A bathtub that gurgles when you flush points past the toilet trap.

Water appears in the lowest drain

A basement floor drain or shower pan that bubbles up is the line's warning.

Toilet bubbles when the washer drains

Air is being pushed back through the same blocked run.

Repeated clogs after plunging

A root-filled or collapsed section can recreate the clog within days.

FAQs

Common questions

How long should I wait after using dish soap?

Give it 10 to 15 minutes to sink, then another 10 after the hot water. If nothing changes, repeat once at most and then switch to a plunger.

Can I use laundry detergent or shampoo instead?

Liquid dish soap is the usual choice because it is mild and rinses easily. Detergents can foam heavily, and bar soap or thick shampoo may not flow down to the clog.

Why does my toilet keep clogging?

Low-flow flushes, too much paper, flushed wipes, or a partial blockage in the drain line are common reasons. A weak flush that never fully clears the bowl may need a look at the flapper and fill valve.

Is a clogged toilet an emergency?

A single clog that is contained is not. Sewage rising in a tub or floor drain is, because it can damage floors and carries contamination. Our 24/7 line is for those cases.
