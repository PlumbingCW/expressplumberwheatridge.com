Basement Waterproofing in Wheat Ridge, CO

Water in a basement has a source, and the fix depends on finding it. We handle the plumbing side: sumps, drains and backwater valves, with honest advice on the rest.

Basement waterproofing in Wheat Ridge covers a mix of trades, and only part of it is plumbing. Waterproofing means keeping groundwater, surface runoff and sewer backup out of a below-grade space, using drains, pumps, sealants and grading.

We are plumbers, so we do the pump and pipe side: sump pumps, interior drain connections, floor drains and backwater valves. Excavation, foundation crack injection and exterior membranes are usually a waterproofing contractor's work, and we will say so when that is what you need.

Symptoms

Where is the basement water coming from?

The source decides the repair, so look before you call anyone.

Puddles along the wall base

Water at the wall and floor joint often points to groundwater pressing against the foundation.

Dark patch near a window well

A full or poorly drained well can funnel rain against the glass and frame.

Chalky white film on masonry

Efflorescence is mineral residue left behind as moisture dries, a sign of ongoing seepage.

Water rising at a floor drain

That usually means a sewer or drain backup, not groundwater, and it needs a plumber.

Pump runs often or fails

A struggling sump pump is a plumbing problem we can test and repair.

What plumbing can fix and what it cannot

A sump pump and basin collect water from perimeter drains and send it outside through a discharge line. We install and repair these, along with their check valves, floats and backup pumps. A pump that works is often the cheapest protection a basement has.

Floor drains and backwater valves guard against sewer backup. A backwater valve is a one-way flap in the sewer line that closes when flow reverses. Installing one means cutting into the slab or line, and permit rules vary, so ask the Wheat Ridge Building Division.

We do not excavate foundations, apply exterior membranes or inject wall cracks. Those jobs belong to a waterproofing contractor. If your problem is seepage through the wall, we can still make sure the pump and drains are ready for the extra water.

Exterior and interior water control, in plain terms

Exterior work usually means digging down to the footing, cleaning and sealing the wall, then adding drain pipe and gravel before backfilling. It is disruptive and costly, so it tends to be saved for serious cases. Grading and downspouts are the cheap first step.

Interior drain tile is perforated pipe laid along the footing inside the basement, set in gravel and running to a sump basin. French drains work on the same idea outdoors, with a trench of gravel and perforated pipe that carries water away from the wall.

Window wells need a drain at the bottom or a cover, or they fill like a bucket. Downspouts should discharge several feet from the house. Before any digging, Colorado 811 must be called at least three business days ahead, and hand tools are required within 18 inches of marked lines.

FAQs

Common questions

My sump pump failed and the basement is taking on water. What now?

Keep clear of standing water near electrical outlets, then call. We repair or replace pumps, check valves and floats, and can add a battery backup.

Do you install French drains?

We can plan the drain, the discharge and the connection to a pump basin. Heavy excavation is often done with a contractor who handles digging and grading. We tell you which parts are ours.

Is a plumber the same as a basement waterproofer?

No. Plumbers work on pumps, pipes, drains and valves. Waterproofers seal walls, install exterior membranes and excavate. Many wet basements need both.

What is a backwater valve and do I need one?

It is a one-way valve in the sewer line that blocks sewage from flowing back into the house. It suits basements with floor drains, toilets or showers below the sewer level. A plumber can check whether yours fits.

Can I just regrade the soil or lengthen downspouts myself?

Yes, those are common DIY steps. Slope soil away from the foundation and extend downspouts several feet. Call 811 before any digging.

Do you work with landlords and property managers?

Yes. We coordinate access with tenants and note what we find, so you have a record of the pump, drain or valve work.
