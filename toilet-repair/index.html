Our 24/7 line is open now, and a licensed plumber can be dispatched to your address. While you wait, stop the water at the wall and keep the bathroom door closed to protect the hallway.

Most toilet failures come down to a few parts: the wax ring, the flapper and fill valve in the tank, the closet flange in the floor, or a clog in the trap. Each needs a different fix.

We repair what can be repaired and replace what cannot, and you hear the price first.

Do this first

When a toilet problem cannot wait until morning

Water on the floor that keeps coming, a bowl rising toward the rim, or a ceiling stain below an upstairs bathroom all count as emergencies. Water from the bowl itself may carry sewage.

  1. Turn the oval handle on the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  2. If the bowl is rising, lift the tank lid and push the rubber flapper down to stop more water entering.
  3. Put towels along the base and keep children and pets out of the room.
  4. Do not flush again, and do not pour drain cleaner into a full bowl.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell us whether the water is clear or dirty.
Symptoms

What your toilet is telling you

Puddle around the base

A failed wax ring or a loose closet bolt lets water escape each time you flush. Water seeping under vinyl or tile can soften the subfloor over time.

Rocking when you sit

A wobbling bowl usually means loose flange bolts or a flange sitting below the finished floor. Tightening alone rarely holds if the flange is damaged.

Tank refills on its own

A worn flapper lets tank water seep into the bowl, so the fill valve keeps topping up. You may hear a faint hiss or see ripples in the bowl.

Running toilet or slow leak: what is actually failing

A toilet that runs after the flush, hisses at night or refills every few minutes can waste a surprising amount of water. Typical culprits are a warped flapper, a chain with too much slack, a tired fill valve or a float set too high so water spills into the overflow tube.

Try the dye test: add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing, and look at the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

Local note

Wheat Ridge water and what it does to toilet parts

Four water providers serve Wheat Ridge, and the city does not say which one feeds a given address. Denver Water reports moderately hard supply in its southern system and softer water in its northern one, with hardness shifting slightly between winter and summer. Minerals harden rubber flappers and scale up fill valve seats.

So check your water bill for the provider name, ask them which system feeds your street, and test your tap. If your toilet parts keep failing every couple of years, scale is a likely reason, and a repair that includes a cleaned valve seat will last longer.

Installers in Colorado work to a model plumbing code, commonly the UPC or IPC family, with local amendments, so the permit desk is the place to confirm what applies at your address.

Options

Repair the toilet or replace it

Repair is usually enough when

  • The bowl and tank are uncracked
  • The leak is at the base, flapper or fill valve
  • The flush is still strong when working
  • The toilet is a standard size that fits the room

Replacement makes more sense when

  • There is a hairline crack in the china
  • The same clog returns after clearing
  • Parts for an older model are hard to find
  • You want a lower-flow or taller bowl

New toilet installs are part of this service. Local permit rules vary, so ask the Wheat Ridge Building Division if your project moves the drain.

FAQs

Common questions

Do you handle toilet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Wheat Ridge?

Yes. The emergency line is staffed 24/7, including weekends and holidays, and same-day appointments are offered when available for less urgent problems. Shut the supply valve, tell us what you see, and we dispatch a licensed plumber.

Why does my toilet leak at the base only after flushing?

The wax ring is often the culprit. It seals the bowl to the flange, and a loose bowl or a flange sitting too low can break that seal under the surge of a flush. The ring is cheap, but the fix needs the bowl pulled.

My toilet rocks. Can you tighten it?

Sometimes. If the bolts are loose, a snug retighten and fresh caps may be all it needs. If the flange is cracked, rusted or below the floor line, we repair or raise it so the new seal holds.

Do you remove the old toilet when you install a new one?

Yes. We disconnect the supply, lift the old bowl out, scrape the flange, set a new seal and connect the new unit. Tell us the rough-in distance from the wall if you already bought one.
