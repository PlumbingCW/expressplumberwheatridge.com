Emergency plumber in Wheat Ridge, day or night

An emergency plumber is a licensed plumber who answers when water is already damaging your home. That means a burst supply line, a sewer backing up through a floor drain, or a water heater leaking onto the basement floor. Our 24/7 line exists for those moments.

Calls are answered around the clock, including weekends and holidays. You describe the problem, and the line connects you to a plumber who handles that kind of job in Wheat Ridge and the towns around it. You get a clear price before work starts, so a midnight repair does not come with a surprise afterward.

Not every plumbing problem is an emergency. A dripping faucet can wait for a same-day appointment when one is available. A line that is pouring water into your walls cannot, and that is when you want an emergency plumber on the way as soon as possible.

Do this first

What to do first when water will not stop

A few minutes of the right action can save a floor, a ceiling or a finished basement. Do these things before you call, and keep the phone in your hand while you work.

  1. Shut off the main water valve, usually near the meter or where the supply line enters the house.
  2. Stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and the washing machine if sewage is backing up.
  3. Clear the area of rugs, electronics and anything that can soak up water.
  4. Stay away from outlets and appliances standing in water.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from.
Local note

Wheat Ridge details that matter for your plumbing

Wheat Ridge began in 1859 as a rest stop for gold miners and later became the Carnation City, with greenhouses that the city says grew the largest carnation crop in the world. The city has grown in stages since then, so two buildings on one street can have very different plumbing. Ask your plumber to identify the pipe material on site instead of assuming.

Clear Creek runs through the city, and Lena Gulch joins it in the southwest. If your property sits near water, mention it on the call so the plumber can ask the right questions about drainage and sump pumps. Do not guess at groundwater, because the facts vary lot by lot.

W 38th Avenue is the main commercial corridor, and our address is 7400 W 38th Ave. Shops and restaurants along it often need commercial plumbing, so tell us if your problem is in a business. The Lutheran Legacy Campus at 8300 W 38th Ave, about 100 acres under a long redevelopment plan, is a reminder to call 811 before any digging nearby.

At Crown Hill Park, the lake level changes each year because of a pump house on the east side. If you see standing water near the park, run a meter test before you blame your own pipes. The Wheat Ridge Historic Park at 44th Avenue and Robb Street holds a farmhouse from about 1904, and older buildings like it deserve a pipe check before you plan a remodel.

Who supplies your water and handles your sewer

Wheat Ridge is a limited-service city, so the city itself does not provide water, fire, sanitation or trash. The city lists four water providers: Denver Water, Consolidated Mutual, Valley Water and the Wheat Ridge Water District. Check your water bill to see which one serves your address.

Sewer service is also split. The Fruitdale Sanitation District covers the northwest part of the city, roughly Ward Road to Kipling Street and 44th Avenue to 52nd Avenue. Other sanitation districts have Wheat Ridge mailing addresses too.

Before you dig or replace a line, ask your district where its responsibility ends and your own lateral begins. That one question can decide who pays for a repair, so make the call early.

FAQs

Questions about emergency plumbing in Wheat Ridge

Is there a plumber available at night in Wheat Ridge?

Yes. The emergency line is answered 24 hours a day, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call, describe the problem, and you will be connected to a licensed emergency plumber.

How do I check that a plumber is licensed in Colorado?

The Colorado State Plumbing Board, part of the Division of Professions and Occupations, runs a public licence lookup on the state website. Search by name and confirm the licence is current. Master, Journeyworker and Residential Plumber licences all appear there.

Will I know the price before the work starts?

Yes. The plumber explains the work and the price first, and you decide whether to go ahead. If the job turns out to be bigger than it looked, you hear about it before more work begins.

What should I do before the plumber arrives?

Shut off the main water valve if water is escaping, and keep people away from wet outlets. Move valuables out of the wet area. Take note of when the problem started so you can describe it clearly.

Do you handle sewer backups?

Yes, we route them to plumbers who do sewer line cleaning and repair. Stop using water in the house first, since every flush or load of laundry adds to the backup.

Can I get a same-day appointment for a non-emergency?

Often, yes. Same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows. A running toilet or slow drain can usually wait for one, while active flooding should be treated as an emergency.

Do you work on commercial buildings?

Yes. Restaurants, shops and offices along W 38th Avenue and elsewhere can call the same line. See our commercial plumbing page for the kinds of jobs we route.

Who do I call if I smell gas?

Leave the building right away, then call 911 from outside. Do not use switches or phones inside. A plumbing line is not the right call for a gas smell.
