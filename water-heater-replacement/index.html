Emergency water heater replacement in Wheat Ridge is the swap of a failed tank for a new unit, including the disconnect, haul-away, new connections and a safe restart. Our 24/7 line is open overnight and on weekends. While you wait for the plumber, shut the cold supply and power to the dead unit so it cannot add to the damage.

Not every cold shower means a new tank. We check first whether a thermostat, element or valve can bring the old one back, and we say so when it can. When the steel shell has rusted through, replacement is the only sound choice, and the decision then moves to size, fuel type and venting.

Do this first

When a failed tank needs attention tonight

A tank that has split can release most of its contents within minutes. Water on the floor with a hot tank above it is a scald and slip risk, and electric units near standing water add a shock hazard.

Gas smell near the unit means you leave the house first, then call 911 and the gas utility from outside.

  1. Close the cold-water inlet valve on top of the heater.
  2. Turn off the breaker for an electric unit, or set a gas control to off.
  3. Do not step into standing water near a plugged-in or wired unit until power is off.
  4. Pull stored items clear and start mopping or wet-vacuuming the edges.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell us the tank's fuel type and size if you can read the label.

A leaking water heater usually means a new tank

Water that seeps from the tank seam or bottom head comes from rust that has eaten through the steel. No sealant or patch holds against 40 to 80 psi of household pressure and daily heating cycles. We confirm first that the water is not coming from a fitting, a relief valve line or condensation.

Once we have ruled out those easier causes, the replacement conversation starts. The failed unit is drained, disconnected and removed, so you are not left with a dead tank in the garage.

Tanks often last roughly 8 to 12 years, but sediment and a spent anode rod can shorten that. If you already replaced elements or a valve twice, the tank is telling you something.

No hot water and the heater is old

When there is simply no hot water, we start with the cheap checks: breaker, reset button, pilot and thermostat. If those pass and the tank is near the end of its range, a new unit often makes more sense than chasing parts.

Choosing the size, fuel and type of the new unit

A water heater is sized by household demand, not by the old label alone. We look at the number of bathrooms, how many people shower in the morning and whether a soaking tub or a second dishwasher run at the same time. A 40-gallon tank suits many small households, while a larger family may want 50 or 75 gallons.

Keeping the same fuel is the simplest swap, since the gas line or 240-volt circuit is already there. Switching from gas to electric, or the reverse, needs new supply work and may need a permit. We will tell you when that step is involved before you commit.

  • First-hour rating matters more than tank capacity alone
  • Footprint and height must fit the closet or alcove
  • Fuel switches mean new gas line, circuit or vent work

A note on heat pump water heaters

Heat pump (hybrid) water heaters pull heat from the surrounding air, so they use much less electricity than a standard electric tank. They are taller than most tanks, need clear space and airflow, and drain condensate. They also cool the room they sit in.

They suit some homes well and others poorly, depending on the space. Ask us about fit and clearances. We do not make claims about rebates or incentives, so check those with your utility directly.

Venting and combustion air for the new tank

Gas water heaters must vent exhaust safely, and the vent path is checked at every swap. We look for proper slope, tight joints, correct pipe diameter and clearance from combustibles. A corroded or disconnected flue is a carbon monoxide risk, and we correct it rather than reconnect it.

Power-vent and direct-vent models use fans and sealed pipes instead of a natural draft. They allow more flexible placement but add a powered part that can fail. Electric and heat pump units need no flue.

Local note

Permits and licensing for a water heater swap in Wheat Ridge

The City of Wheat Ridge licenses plumbing contractors under Class 4, and that licence covers water heater installation. A building permit is required for alterations, but permit rules vary, so ask the Wheat Ridge Building Division whether your swap needs one.

When a plumbing contractor does the work, the contractor handles the permit. State rules also bar unregistered people other than the homeowner from installing plumbing on a property. Ask any plumber for the licence, and you can look it up through the State Plumbing Board.

Pipe sizing, venting and trap rules come from model plumbing codes like the UPC and IPC, which each city adopts in its own form, so permit rules vary and the Building Division has the answer. Any dedicated circuit follows the National Electrical Code (NEC) as adopted locally, so the electrical permit is separate from the plumbing one.

FAQs

Common questions

Do you handle water heater replacement emergencies at night or on weekends in Wheat Ridge?

Yes. The 24/7 line is staffed for failed tanks at any hour, and a licensed plumber can come out to shut off and assess. Whether the new unit is set that night or the next day depends on what we need to fetch and the schedule.

How do I know if my tank can be repaired instead?

A leak through the tank wall means replacement. A heating fault, bad thermostat or tripped limit switch on a sound tank is usually a repair. We test before recommending anything.

Can I switch from a tank to a tankless unit?

Often yes, but it is a bigger project: gas line sizing, venting and sometimes electrical work. Our tankless water heater page explains the trade-offs.

What size water heater do I need?

It depends on peak demand, not just the number of people. Bathrooms, simultaneous showers and appliances all count. We check the first-hour rating of candidate units against your household's busiest hour.

Will you remove my old water heater?

Yes. The old tank is drained, disconnected and taken away as part of the job, unless you tell us otherwise.

Do I need a permit?

Permit rules vary, so ask the Wheat Ridge Building Division. When we do the work as a licensed plumbing contractor, we take care of the permit side.

I manage rentals. Can you replace a tenant's failed heater?

Yes. We can coordinate access with the tenant and give you the price up front for approval. Landlords with several properties can call the same 24/7 line.
